# Evacuation Protocols, Route Safety & Shelter Management

This document provides life-safety evacuation rules, route navigation standards, and shelter management principles for field operators, responders, and citizens using FloodGuard AI.

---

## 1. Life-Safety Evacuation Principles in Mountain Terrain

1. **Immediate Vertical Evacuation**:
   - In steep mountain valleys, horizontal distance matters less than **vertical elevation**.
   - Ascend immediately to higher ground at least **30 to 50 meters above the active river/channel bed**.
   - Never attempt to flee downstream along the river valley road; flash flood wave velocities ($5 - 8\text{ m/s}$) frequently outrun vehicles and pedestrians on winding ghat roads.

2. **The "Turn Around Don't Drown" Rule**:
   - **15 cm (6 inches)** of rapidly moving water can sweep an adult off their feet.
   - **30 cm (12 inches)** of rushing water will float small cars.
   - **60 cm (24 inches)** of turbulent water will carry away heavy SUVs and trucks.
   - Never walk, cycle, or drive through flooded causeways, bridges, or culverts.

3. **Bridge & Culvert Choke Hazards**:
   - Mountain culverts and narrow arched bridges become choked with logs, boulders, and debris.
   - Choking creates temporary upstream dams followed by sudden catastrophic structural blowouts.
   - Treat any submerged bridge approach as impassable and structurally compromised until inspected by engineers.

## 2. Evacuation Route Classification in FloodGuard AI

FloodGuard AI evaluates candidate escape routes using real-time spatial elevation and risk contours:

- **RECOMMENDED / CANDIDATE (Green)**:
  - Route maintains a continuous upward elevation profile (+50m to +150m above base level).
  - Path traverses ridge lines or upper valley slopes away from alluvial channels.
  - No active landslide scars or inundated culverts detected along the segment.

- **DEGRADED / CAUTION (Amber)**:
  - Route passes within 100 meters of a swelling tributary or high TWI zone.
  - Unpaved surface susceptible to mud accumulation; 4x4 or foot transit recommended.

- **BLOCKED / IMPASSABLE (Red)**:
  - Direct intersection with an active flood surge channel, mudflow, or submerged culvert.
  - Bridge washed out or approach collapsed.
  - Operators must immediately broadcast blockades and redirect evacuees to secondary ridge trails.

## 3. Designated Emergency Shelters

Standard public buildings identified for emergency relief camps:

1. **Government Senior Secondary Schools & Colleges**:
   - Multi-story masonry or RCC structures located on elevated plateaus.
   - Built-in sanitation, potable water tanks, and large assembly halls (capacity: $250 - 600$ persons).

2. **Gram Panchayat Bhawans & Community Centers**:
   - Local administrative nodes equipped with backup power (solar/DG) and wireless communication (capacity: $100 - 250$ persons).

3. **Cyclone & Flood Multi-Purpose Shelters (MPCS)**:
   - Engineered specifically for disaster resistance with stilt elevation and helipad rooftops (capacity: $500 - 1,000$ persons).

## 4. Priority Evacuation & Vulnerable Group Care

Responders must organize evacuation into triaged waves:

- **Wave 1 (High Priority)**:
  - Bedridden patients, elderly individuals, pregnant women, and infants.
  - Persons with physical, visual, or cognitive disabilities.
- **Wave 2**:
  - General population, children, and school groups.
- **Wave 3**:
  - Emergency response team securing utility shutoffs (electricity main grid, LPG cylinders).
- **Livestock Protocol**:
  - Untie cattle and goats before evacuating. Tethered animals perish in flash surges; free animals instinctively climb higher ground.
