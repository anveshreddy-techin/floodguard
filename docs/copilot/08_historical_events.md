# Verified Historical Disaster Events (Hindcast Benchmark Archive)

This document contains authoritative technical, physical, and observational reconstructions of major flood and mass-wasting disasters in South Asia. These case studies provide the validation benchmark for FloodGuard AI models.

---

## 1. 2013 Kedarnath / Uttarakhand Multi-Day Disaster
- **Date**: June 15–17, 2013
- **Primary Geography**: Mandakini Basin, Rudraprayag & Chamoli Districts, Uttarakhand, India
- **Coordinates**: 30.735° N, 79.067° E (Kedarnath Shrine)
- **Primary Triggers**:
  1. Early arrival of the Southwest Monsoon intersecting with a mid-latitude Westerly trough, dumping > 350 mm of rain in 48 hours.
  2. Substantial early-season snowpack melting.
  3. Breaching of the lateral moraine dam of Chorabari Lake (Gandhi Sarovar) at ~3,900m on the morning of June 17, releasing an estimated $400,000\text{ m}^3$ of impounded water in under 15 minutes.
- **Physical Dynamics**:
  - The debris-laden surge wave descended at velocities exceeding $7 - 9\text{ m/s}$ along a 14° bed slope.
  - River discharge at downstream stations (Rudraprayag confluence with Alaknanda) surged beyond historical records, carrying massive granitic boulders that pulverized downstream settlements (Rambara, Gaurikund, Sonprayag).
- **Casualties & Impact**: Official death toll exceeding 5,700 people; complete destruction of pedestrian access and Rambara town.
- **FloodGuard Teachable Lessons**:
  - Highlights why single-station rain gauges fail without high-altitude moraine lake monitoring.
  - Demonstrates necessity of modeling antecedent snowmelt + rainfall interaction.

## 2. 2021 Chamoli Rock & Ice Avalanche (Rishiganga & Dhauliganga)
- **Date**: February 7, 2021 (~10:21 AM IST)
- **Primary Geography**: Rishiganga and Dhauliganga valleys, Chamoli District, Uttarakhand, India
- **Coordinates**: 30.380° N, 79.730° E (Ronti Peak source)
- **Primary Trigger**:
  - Sudden structural detachment of approximately 27 million cubic meters of rock and hanging glacier ice from the north face of Ronti Peak (~5,600m ASL).
  - **Crucial Feature**: Zero precipitation trigger. The day was sunny and clear with no rainfall.
- **Physical Dynamics**:
  - The wedge dropped ~1,800m into the Ronti Gad gorge, pulverizing ice and rock. Friction melted the ice into water, transforming the mass into a hyper-mobile debris avalanche and flash flood wave.
  - The surge reached heights of over 20 meters at the Rishiganga Hydropower Project (13.2 MW) and inundated the Tapovan-Vishnugad Hydropower Project (520 MW) tunnels downstream within 20 minutes.
- **Casualties & Impact**: Over 200 workers and local villagers dead or missing; two major hydroelectric projects destroyed.
- **FloodGuard Teachable Lessons**:
  - Rainfall models alone cannot predict cryogenic/geotechnical failures.
  - Demonstrates why Tier D (Isolation Forest) vibration/geophone anomaly screening and thermal satellite alerts are essential.

## 3. 2021 Melamchi Flash Flood & Debris Flow (Nepal)
- **Date**: June 15, 2021
- **Primary Geography**: Melamchi River Basin, Sindhupalchok District, Bagmati Province, Nepal
- **Primary Trigger**:
  - Intense localized precipitation in the upper headwaters (Bremdang and Pemdang catchments) causing high-altitude moraine collapses, damming of tributary channels, and subsequent chain-reaction breaches.
- **Physical Dynamics**:
  - Successive debris dams formed by landslides failed in cascading pulses, mobilizing millions of tons of sediment and logs down the Melamchi River.
  - Destroyed the headworks of the landmark Melamchi Water Supply Project and inundated Melamchi Bazaar under 5 to 10 meters of mud and debris.
- **Casualties & Impact**: Dozens missing/killed; hundreds of homes buried; over $200 million in infrastructural losses.
- **FloodGuard Teachable Lessons**:
  - Cascading hazards where initial slope failure dams a stream, creating an artificial reservoir that later bursts with exponential force.

## 4. 2023 North Sikkim GLOF (South Lhonak Lake & Teesta Surge)
- **Date**: October 3–4, 2023 (~midnight)
- **Primary Geography**: South Lhonak Lake and Teesta River Basin, Mangan & Pakyong Districts, Sikkim, India
- **Coordinates**: 27.91° N, 88.20° E (South Lhonak Lake, 5,200m ASL)
- **Primary Trigger**:
  - Sudden moraine failure of the proglacial South Lhonak Lake (which had expanded significantly over preceding decades), potentially triggered by heavy rain and lateral moraine ice avalanche displacement.
- **Physical Dynamics**:
  - The lake drained over 60% of its volume in hours. The surge raced down the Lachen and Lachung valleys into the Teesta River.
  - Completely destroyed the 1,200 MW Chungthang Dam (Teesta-III), sweeping away military cantonments at Bardang and severing the vital NH-10 arterial highway.
- **Casualties & Impact**: Over 100 casualties (including Indian Army personnel); extensive infrastructure loss across Sikkim and northern West Bengal.
- **FloodGuard Teachable Lessons**:
  - Proves the life-or-death value of downstream early warning sirens linked directly to high-altitude water level threshold transceivers.

## 5. 2024 Wayanad Landslides & Flash Floods (Western Ghats)
- **Date**: July 30, 2024 (~02:00 AM IST)
- **Primary Geography**: Chooralmala, Mundakkai, and Attamala, Meppadi Panchayat, Wayanad District, Kerala, India
- **Coordinates**: 11.530° N, 76.170° E
- **Primary Trigger**:
  - Consecutive days of extraordinarily intense orographic precipitation exceeding 572 mm in 48 hours on steep, pre-saturated laterite tea plantation slopes.
- **Physical Dynamics**:
  - Multiple slope liquefaction events in the upper Vellarimala hills coalesced into a massive debris flow of water, mud, and boulders traveling down the Iruvazhinji riverbed.
  - The flow swept away the Chooralmala bridge, cutting off rescue access to Mundakkai, and buried residential settlements under thick sludge.
- **Casualties & Impact**: Over 300 fatalities; complete devastation of three village centers.
- **FloodGuard Teachable Lessons**:
  - Illustrates the critical role of the Antecedent Precipitation Index (API) and soil saturation index in Western Ghats hazard calculation.

## 6. 2005 Mumbai Cloudburst & Urban Flood
- **Date**: July 26, 2005
- **Primary Geography**: Mumbai Metropolitan Region, Maharashtra, India
- **Trigger**: 944 mm of rain recorded in 24 hours at Santacruz observatory.
- **Dynamics**: Coincided with a high tide in the Arabian Sea, preventing the Mithi River from discharging into the sea, resulting in massive urban submergence and transport paralysis.

## 7. 2014 Kashmir Jhelum Basin Flood
- **Date**: September 2–7, 2014
- **Primary Geography**: Jhelum River Valley, Srinagar and South Kashmir, Jammu & Kashmir, India
- **Trigger**: Prolonged extreme rainfall across Pir Panjal catchment.
- **Dynamics**: Jhelum River breached embankments at multiple locations, submerging 70% of Srinagar city for weeks.

## 8. 2015 Chennai Inundation
- **Date**: November–December 2015
- **Primary Geography**: Chennai, Kanchipuram, and Tiruvallur Districts, Tamil Nadu, India
- **Trigger**: Record Northeast Monsoon depressions dumping > 490 mm in 24 hours on December 1.
- **Dynamics**: Synchronized emergency discharge from Chembarambakkam Reservoir into the already swollen Adyar River, inundating residential neighborhoods.

## 9. 2023 Himachal Pradesh Monsoon Floods
- **Date**: July 8–11 & August 12–15, 2023
- **Primary Geography**: Beas, Sutlej, and Ravi Basins (Kullu, Mandi, Shimla), Himachal Pradesh, India
- **Trigger**: Cloudbursts combined with western disturbance monsoonal convergence (> 250 mm in 24h).
- **Dynamics**: Unprecedented river stage in the Beas River carrying cars, buildings, and bridges in Manali and Pandoh.
